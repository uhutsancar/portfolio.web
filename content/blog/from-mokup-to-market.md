Successful products rarely come from a single perfect idea.

They are usually the result of research, experimentation,
testing, and continuous collaboration between designers,
developers, stakeholders, and users.

## Discovery and research

The first step is understanding the actual problem before
designing a solution.

This phase includes stakeholder interviews, user research,
competitor analysis, existing product reviews, and the
definition of measurable project goals.

## User flows and wireframes

After collecting the research, the findings are transformed
into user journeys, information architecture, and low-fidelity
wireframes.

Wireframes help validate structure and functionality before
visual design decisions are introduced.

## Prototyping and testing

Interactive prototypes allow users and stakeholders to
experience the proposed product before development begins.

Testing at this stage helps identify confusing navigation,
missing feedback, unnecessary steps, and unclear content.

## Visual design

Once the primary experience has been validated, the visual
system is created.

Typography, color, spacing, imagery, component states, and
responsive behavior are designed as parts of a consistent
system rather than as isolated screens.

## Developer handoff

The final stage includes close collaboration with developers.

Responsive behavior, interaction states, component variations,
design tokens, and accessibility expectations are documented
clearly to make implementation reliable and consistent.
